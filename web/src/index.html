    Plug in a controller and press one of its buttons. Keys and buttons can be changed in the game: Options, Controls. On a phone or tablet the touch controls show over the game: held sideways it fills the screen, upright the picture is on top.

    No profiles yet. Make one in the game: it is saved in this browser.

      Your profiles and settings stay in this browser, never on the server. A backup keeps them safe, or takes them to another browser.

       

      Preloaded, the game reads its tracks, cars and music from this device instead of the server: no waiting on a slow connection. Play stops the download; what came stays, and Preload fetches the rest later.

      You are playing from this computer's own server, so there is little to gain here.

      The game's own Options, Graphics, here before you play. A change applies at the next Play; in the game you change the same things the same way, and the two stay in step. Saved in this browser.

      The race fills a wide window, but the menus keep the shape they were made in, 4:3, so a wide window has bars on both sides. This fills them with a soft copy of the menu instead of black. Off by default: it may not look right everywhere, and a movie's own dark edge can show against it.

      Need for Speed: Hot Pursuit 2 (2002), the PlayStation 2 version, in your browser. The game's own code is translated to C++ ahead of time and compiled to WebAssembly; WebGPU draws it and Web Audio plays it.

      The driving is the console's: the demo races recorded in the game replay with every one of their checks matching.

      Needs the latest Chrome or Edge on a computer, or the latest Chrome on an Android phone.

      The site counts visits, plays and time played to see how it is doing: no cookies, and no addresses or anything about you kept.
